Birds Across Australia

What do Australia's bird records reveal?

Explore where birds are recorded across Australian states and seasons. Discover their habitats, species and their conservation challenges.

Where was the widest variety of birds recorded?

Queensland stands out even after we level the comparison. Looking at 500 records per state, it shows the widest variety of recorded birds.

170species expected from 500 Queensland records

NSW follows at about 155 species. That gap matters because seeing the same bird repeatedly adds reports, not new species.

Results still depend on where birds were recorded.

So variety and recording effort tell different stories. Which state submitted the most records?

Did more bird records mean more bird species?

Counting reports changes the picture. NSW submitted far more sightings than Queensland, despite Queensland's richer 500-record sample.

2.2×as many bird records in NSW as Queensland

Victoria also logged 19,826 records. Repeated sightings of familiar birds can make a state look busier without adding more kinds of birds.

These totals reflect reporting activity as well as which birds people happened to observe.

So which species account for those reports?

Were the same birds recorded frequently in every state?

Every state has its own mix of recorded birds. Australian Magpies feature strongly in Victoria and NSW. Tasmania's story looks different.

336Forest Raven records in Tasmania, versus just 4 in NSW

Western Australia tells another story, with 434 Australian Raven records. The same species do not dominate every state's sightings.

These six familiar species show how different the state patterns can be. They are a selected comparison, not a complete bird checklist.

Even pale tiles need care: few recorded sightings do not prove a bird was absent. Would these patterns change with the seasons?

How does Australia's bird calendar change throughout the year?

Bird sightings change with the seasons. On Birdfact’s selected list, November had 104 regularly recorded species, while June had 83.

That seasonal rise was not equal everywhere. NSW had 17 more regularly recorded species in November than June, but ACT had only four more.

Beyond sightings: habitats and Australia-only birds

Where birds live adds another layer to the story. Open landscapes lead the species checklist, with forests close behind.

60.6%of listed species are associated with open landscapes or forests

Coasts, wetlands and rivers also account for many species. Each bird is counted under its main habitat here, showing how varied these environments are.

This mix of habitats is only part of the story. Some birds are found across the world, while others occur only in Australia.

Which of these birds occur only in Australia?

Bird groups differ greatly in where they occur. Many selected parrots and cockatoos occur only here, while shorebirds often range farther.

78.9%of 57 selected parrot and cockatoo species occur only in Australia

Perching birds also show a high Australia-only share. By contrast, none of the 50 selected petrels and albatrosses was classified as Australia-only.

Being recorded in Australia does not always mean a bird is unique to Australia. Some groups have a much wider reach.

That raises another question: do birds found only here have different main habitats?

Habitat reveals a clear contrast between the groups. Open landscapes feature far more among Australia-only species than among species also found elsewhere.

31.6percentage-point difference between the two species groups

At the coast, the pattern reverses. Seas and coasts are the main habitat for 32.4% of elsewhere-occurring species, versus 2.8% of Australia-only species.

The 0% for rocky and arid habitats means that none of the 296 selected species also found elsewhere has this as its main habitat.

It does not mean that no birds live in these places. What threats do these birds face?

Threatened birds: risk across Australia

A familiar habitat does not guarantee a safe future. By 2020, more Australian bird species appeared in threatened categories. The growing list raises wider conservation concerns.

+36 more assessed threatened species and subspecies than in 2010

Vulnerable rose from 68 to 87, while Endangered climbed from 60 to 76. Critically Endangered changed very little, moving from 20 to 21. These categories did not grow equally.

These figures describe changes in assessments, not counts of individual birds. To understand what has happened to monitored bird populations, we need to look at their relative abundance across regions.

Bird declines were widespread but uneven. Every reporting region saw falling relative abundance among monitored threatened and near-threatened birds from 2000 to 2020.

−69.6%average decline among monitored birds in South Australia

Queensland followed at 65.7% and NSW with the ACT at 56.3%. The Northern Territory recorded a smaller 3.4% decline.

These are changes in monitored bird abundance, not counts of birds lost. Where were threatened birds recorded? NSW offers a closer look.

From regional trends to recorded sightings. These April 2025 NSW observations involve birds classified as threatened in a 2015 assessment.

Only 100 recorded locations are plotted, with one point per small area to make overlapping sightings easier to see.

Many points appear in eastern NSW, but this also reflects recording activity. Dots show sightings, not population size.

Do later assessments show that these birds are recovering? The final chart looks at extinction risk.

Has extinction risk improved for Australian birds?

Improvement was rare. Only 25 of 199 eligible Australian bird species and subspecies showed reduced extinction risk in at least one decade between 1990 and 2020.

12.6%showed lower extinction risk at least once

Among the other 174, 100 faced worsening risk and 74 remained in the same risk category.

Some improvements were temporary, and not every gain was caused by conservation. A lower risk category in one decade does not guarantee lasting recovery.

Those purple squares show progress is possible, but they remain a small part of the picture. Protecting these gains is the next challenge.

Key Takeaways

Variety and sightings differ

Queensland showed the widest variety in equal-sized samples, although NSW had the most records. The busiest state was not the most diverse in this comparison.

November brings more birds

Every state and territory had more regularly recorded species in November than in June. NSW showed the largest increase.

Australia-only birds stand out

Parrots and cockatoos had the highest Australia-only share among the groups shown. Open landscapes were especially common among Australia-only birds.

Recovery remains limited

Threatened-category totals increased, monitored birds declined across regions and relatively few eligible bird taxa showed lower extinction risk.
